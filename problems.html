Problems solved

A business messaging platform with no backend yet
Zoho · Arattai Business Platform
+

Problem

Arattai needed a business-facing platform: multi-tenant accounts, message workflows, and a way to meter and bill usage. None of it existed.

Constraints

Had to scale from zero to production volume without a rewrite, keep per-tenant isolation, and make credit accounting correct under concurrent sends and retries.

Approach

Designed and built the data model, microservice boundaries, message workflows and the credit management system end to end. Treated credits as a ledger with idempotent debits so retries never double-charge, and kept tenant identity on every event.

Outcome

Runs at millions of messages in production.

Every new messaging channel was a new integration
Zoho · CPaaS framework
+

Problem

Each channel (WhatsApp, Arattai Business, Telegram, LINE, WeChat, Viber, Facebook Messenger and Instagram) arrived with its own payloads, delivery semantics, media handling and rate limits. Product teams had to learn each one, and onboarding a channel meant touching many services.

Constraints

Existing consumers could not break. Channel-specific features (templates, interactive messages, read receipts) still had to be reachable, not flattened away.

Approach

Designed a unified, omnichannel framework with one RESTful API surface and a per-channel adapter layer. Common concerns (identity, status tracking, retries, webhooks) live once in the core; channel quirks live in adapters behind a stable contract.

Outcome

Eight channels now sit behind one API. New channels follow a standard onboarding path, and internal products integrate once instead of per channel.

Adding real-time voice to a messaging system
Zoho · WhatsApp Business Calling
+

Problem

Messaging is asynchronous and durable; calling is synchronous and latency-sensitive. WhatsApp Business Calling had to live inside the same product and the same conversation thread.

Constraints

WebRTC signalling, media negotiation and call state all had to be modelled alongside messages, and failures had to degrade gracefully rather than drop the conversation.

Approach

Designed the WebRTC-based infrastructure: call lifecycle and data model, signalling APIs, scaling plan, test strategy and production rollout. Calls appear inline in the thread next to messages.

Outcome

Real-time calling shipped as part of the messaging platform rather than as a separate product. I later rebuilt a version of this in the open in Nudgeway.

Broadcasts capped at 25 messages per second
Zoho · broadcast pipeline
+

Problem

Customers sending campaigns to large audiences were throttled to 25 messages per second for the whole application. A broadcast to a big list took hours, and one tenant's campaign queued behind another's.

Constraints

Each channel enforces its own per-sender rate limits, so throughput had to scale per phone number, not just globally. Ordering per recipient, retries and delivery-status tracking had to keep working at the higher rate.

Approach

Reworked the broadcast path to partition work per sending number, with per-number rate limiters, parallel consumers and back-pressure into Kafka so the pipeline degrades to queueing rather than dropping.

Outcome

Application-level throughput went from 25 to 550 messages per second, and each phone number can sustain 500 messages per second on its own.

Regressions that only showed up against real carriers
Zoho · acceptance testing
+

Problem

Unit tests passed, but behaviour depended on carrier and partner APIs that are slow, rate-limited and sometimes unavailable in test environments. Bugs surfaced in production.

Constraints

Tests had to run in CI without external credentials and still exercise real request and response shapes, including error paths.

Approach

Authored an end-to-end acceptance suite with JUnit and WireMock that mocks carrier and partner APIs with recorded contracts, covering happy paths, timeouts and malformed responses.

Outcome

Fewer production regressions and a contract that partner changes can be checked against.
